# White Beryl: lunar-mansion findings for WorldSystem

Prepared 29 September 2026 from the two Tibetan text files supplied by the project owner. This audit supersedes the earlier provisional Tibetan catalogue. It supports **all 28 mansions as an optional layer of source-based symbolic representations**, with White Beryl as the principal source and Mipham as a separately attributed interpretive source.

## Source identification and method

- **U**: `VEIE0OPI51524892_I1KG12906_bo,sa,zh.txt`, upper volume of Sangye Gyatso's *White Beryl*. The title identifies the Phug tradition (phug lugs).
- **L**: `UTIE0OPI51524892_I1KG12907_bo,sa,zh.txt`, lower volume. Chapter 33 contains the individual mansion treatments. This file also contains a **separate appended work by Mipham**, beginning at printed p. 474.
- [Raw evidence extracts](WHITE-BERYL-SOURCE-EXTRACTS.txt) preserve original line numbers, source-file hashes and OCR readings. U/L citations below refer to the original files. Printed pages are identified from the OCR page footers, not independently inspected page images.

Both files were searched for names, counts, shapes, elements, ordering and relevant attribution. Focused passages were read; this is not a full translation of both volumes. The supplied text is OCR with missing lines. Clear readings below can be used now; damaged local readings are identified individually. English translations are working translations made for this audit. Sanskrit labels are identification concordances, not claims that each Sanskrit spelling occurs in the quoted Tibetan.

## 1. The collection is explicitly twenty-eight

U p. 18, lines 607–608:

> རྒྱུ་བའི་སྐར་མ་ཉི་ཤུ་བརྒྱད། །འོན་ཀྱང་གྲོ་བཞིན་བྱི་བཞིན་གཉིས། །ལོངས་སྤྱོད་གཅིག་པས་བསྡུ་བ་ན། །རྩ་བདུན་ཡིན་ནོ་

Working translation: “The lunar mansions are twenty-eight. However, gro bzhin and byi bzhin share one allotment; when combined, they are twenty-seven.” Here *longs spyod* is rendered as the technical allotment/occupancy, not ordinary enjoyment.

L p. 313, line 10129, explicitly introduces the favorable and unfavorable results of the twenty-eight lunar mansions. The ensuing treatments occupy pp. 313–328. **There is no need for a prominent count debate in the app.** Keep the paired-count explanation in a short source note. A ring of 28 symbolic representations is directly justified as a collection; their equal visual spacing would be an app design choice, not a recovered astronomical division.

U p. 35, lines 1101–1105 also describes the pair in terms of half-allotments within a particular Rāhu/directional procedure, using seven mansions per direction and beginning at smin drug. That passage should not be generalized into universal sky coordinates or the rotation direction of the app.

## 2. Follow this Tibetan order

The descriptive sequence starts with **dbyu gu**, an alias for **tha skar** explicitly supplied in U pp. 18–19. Its latter part is:

**chu stod → chu smad → gro bzhin → byi bzhin → mon dre → mon gru → khrums stod → khrums smad → nam gru.**

L p. 325 treats gro bzhin at line 10503 and then byi bzhin at line 10518. Use **Śravaṇa before Abhijit** in this White Beryl catalogue. The earlier dossier's Indian-style ordering is superseded. Use **mon dre**, the spelling visible repeatedly in both supplied volumes; retain mon gre as a search alias only. The unstacked spellings snon and snubs below follow readable source occurrences; snron and snrubs remain concordance aliases.

### Local reading notes

- **lha mtshams:** the text clearly reads *glang po*, naturally translated as **elephant**. This differs from the “bull” in the earlier German secondary table. The [UMA Tibetan–Sanskrit–English dictionary](https://uma-tibet.org/pdf/greatbooks/UMA_Dictionary_May2016.pdf), printed p. 87, distinguishes *glang* (bull) and *glang po* (elephant). Prefer an elephant for the text-derived model; a manuscript illustration would establish that witness's visual treatment. Do not merge it with byi bzhin's *glang mgo*, ox head.
- **sa ga:** the OCR reads *ri mgo* where the secondary table gives *ra mgo*, goat head. Four stars are legible. The goat reading is a plausible correction, not a verified transcription.
- **rgyal:** the fragment *ril ba'i dbyibs* survives. A rounded form is a cautious working interpretation. The earlier “drop” should not be promoted to an exact source translation from this fragment alone.
- **mon gru:** *me tog phung*, a heap or bunch of flowers, is clear. The same opening also compares it with a person-like form; the syntax around that alternative is damaged. A floral sculpture is supported without resolving the alternative.
- The lost opening motifs are **snar ma, skag, nag pa and khrums smad**. The [secondary Tibetan mansion table](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetische_Mondh%C3%A4user) supplies cart, serpent hood, lotus fruit and ear respectively. It also supplies the missing counts: 5, 6, 1 and 2; rgyal 3, gre 2 and mon gru 2. Keep these as secondary-supported values until the page images are checked. They can guide models with discreet provenance; do not label them directly verified from these OCR lines.

These are specific transcription/image checks. They do not undermine the 28-member collection or the complete Tibetan sequence.

## 4. Elements and directions are now grounded in the supplied text

The source explicitly distinguishes the Indian-style four-element assignments from the Chinese-style five-element assignments incorporated into Tibetan calculation (L p. 334). They describe different classifications. For example, **me bzhi is wind in the four-element list and fire in the five-element list**; **mon gru is earth and water respectively**. Preserve two named fields.

U pp. 257–258, lines 9430–9443, additionally supports this directional grouping:

| Direction | Mansions | Five-element association |
| --- | --- | --- |
| East | smin drug, snar ma, mgo, lag, nabs so, rgyal | wood |
| Southeast | skag | earth (confirmed by L:10811–10812) |
| South | mchu, gre, dbo, me bzhi, nag pa, sa ri | fire |
| Southwest | sa ga | earth |
| West | lha mtshams, snon, snubs, chu stod, chu smad, gro bzhin | iron |
| Northwest | byi bzhin | earth |
| North | mon dre, mon gru, khrums stod, khrums smad, nam gru, dbyu gu | water |
| Northeast | bra nye | earth |

**bra nye is northeast here**, correcting the secondary table's northwest. This is a source-specific elemental arrangement. If used in WorldSystem, label it a traditional directional arrangement and map it deliberately to the app's established east, south, west and north. A gallery with a different starting point need not alter these metadata.

The same passage places the mansions in the cosmological imagery of the tortoise's twenty-eight vertebrae. This supports contextual reading about elemental cosmology; it does not require adding a tortoise to every mansion sculpture.

## 5. Seven planetary rulers, distinct from associated deities

L p. 376, lines 12168–12173 assigns **four mansions to each of seven planetary rulers**:

| Ruler | Mansions |
| --- | --- |
| Mars | smin drug, lha mtshams, mon dre, mchu |
| Mercury | snar ma, gre, snon, mon gru |
| Jupiter | mgo, dbo, snubs, khrums stod |
| Venus | lag, me bzhi, chu stod, khrums smad |
| Saturn | nabs so, nag pa, chu smad, nam gru |
| Sun | rgyal, sa ri, gro bzhin, dbyu gu |
| Moon | skag, sa ga, byi bzhin, bra nye |

This gives a source-grounded Tibetan field. Do not substitute a modern nine-lord nakṣatra cycle.

The individual mansion passages also name deities, a separate association. For example, **byi bzhin** has an ox-head shape, names **Brahmā** (*tshangs pa*, L:10518–10519), is **earth** in both element lists, and has the **Moon** as planetary ruler in this table. Those are four different facts. The phrase *rlung za* in its entry concerns food (“eats wind”), not its element.

Similarly, **mchu** names Saturn as deity at L:10291, while the later rulership list assigns it Mars. Preserve both source roles; do not treat them as an error to be normalized away. The surviving deity names alone do not prescribe bodies, faces, arms, costumes, mounts or colors.

Electional recommendations are also activity-specific. The entries contain favorable and unfavorable actions, and L:12156–12159 weighs interacting factors. These should become short attributed explanations if wanted, not one universal luck score or personalized predictions.

## 6. The supplied volume contains a concrete Mipham connection

A separate appended text begins at **L p. 474, lines 15368–15372**:

**ནག་རྩིས་བརྗེད་བྱང་སྙིང་པོ་གསལ་བ།**  
*nag rtsis brjed byang snying po gsal ba* — a working English title is “Notes on Elemental Astrology, Clarifying the Essence.”

Its opening explicitly says it will clarify some difficult points in the Phug-tradition White Beryl. Its colophon at **L p. 539, line 17723**, states *mi pham pas bris pa*, “written by Mipham.” The title is independently listed in volume 26 of the [Dhagpo Library catalogue of Mipham's collected works](https://dhagpo-library.org/attachments/article/45/mipham_sungbum_wylie.pdf), PDF p. 11.

This supplies a real basis for the user's Nyingma preference: **White Beryl's catalogue read with Mipham's clarificatory notes**. It is more accurate than relabelling Sangye Gyatso's work itself “the Nyingma system.” In the inspected appendix passages I did not find a replacement set of 28 shapes or a complete alternative deity-image prescription.

At **L p. 512, line 16729**, Mipham's appendix enumerates twenty-eight stars and twenty-eight minor stars (the first numeral's OCR ends with བརྒྱུད rather than བརྒྱད). It therefore corroborates use of the 28-member collection in this context. At pp. 527–528 it discusses starting points and direction of counting for a particular calculation; this is not a universal instruction for a celestial ring.

Keep this appendix's attribution separate throughout. Earlier references in White Beryl to **mi pham dge legs** should not automatically be identified with Ju Mipham merely because they share a name.

## 7. Consequences for the revised app and Claude brief

The owner's subsequent revision changes the render format to **2D sprites/billboards inside the existing 3D world**, and adds a separately labeled Indian Lahiri calculator. The source findings above remain applicable.

1. Deliver all 28 in the Tibetan descriptive sequence, with an optional remembered layer, off by default.
2. Draw distinct transparent glyphs from the audited shapes. Color, ornament and drawing style are artistic choices.
3. Use source-specific elemental directions for an illustrative arrangement if helpful; keep computed longitudes separate.
4. Preserve star counts in metadata and detailed views without crowding small glyphs.
5. Provide a complete accessible HTML index alongside scene selection.
6. Keep Tibetan and Sanskrit names, source forms and separately labeled associations readable. Put textual variants and paired counts in expandable sources.
7. Use Mipham's clarifications where applicable, retaining his separate attribution.
8. Retain a small accessible provenance indicator for secondary-supported motifs. Deity names and remedial effigies do not supply complete deity portraits.
9. Keep the Indian calculator's sequence and Vimshottari lord separate from the Tibetan catalogue's order and seven-ruler assignment. The calculator's Lahiri method is not attributed to White Beryl.

See the [revised plan](LUNAR-MANSIONS-PLAN.md) and [technical review](LUNAR-MANSIONS-REVISION-REVIEW.md) for implementation requirements.

U p. 191, lines 7268–7271 also confirms use of **1,620 mansion-time units** for the circuit. This does not mean a physical circle of 1,620 ordinary degrees. It need not appear in the initial layer's UI.

Remaining checks are limited: recover damaged opening lines from printed pp. 315, 317, 319–320 and 326–327; inspect p. 322 for sa ga; consult actual illustrations before claiming historical star-node geometry or complete deity iconography. The principal content and model direction can proceed now.
